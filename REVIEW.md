## Reading the Challenge

Read the five declarations in [Challenge.lean](Challenge.lean) against §1. All groups are finite. The hypotheses on the generating set say it is inverse-closed, excludes the identity and generates the group. With these hypotheses each vertex has degree `S.ncard`. Edges join a group element to its **right** multiple by a generator. The group acts on the graph by **left** multiplication.

* **Theorem 1.1** is `odd_order_cayley_contains_two_power_cycle`. The input is an arbitrary finite group of odd order and a symmetric generating set of cardinality at least three. The witness is a simple closed walk with exactly `2 ^ n` edges and `2 ≤ n`. There is no solvability assumption in the statement.
* **The stronger conclusion immediately after Theorem 1.1** is `noncyclic_odd_order_symmetric_figure_eight`. Its size bound on the generating set is automatic. In odd order each nonidentity generator is distinct from its inverse, and a single inverse pair can generate only a cyclic group. The predicate `HasSymmetricFigureEight` asks for two simple cycles of equal power-of-two length with a unique common vertex. Each walk is based there. Deleting the first entry of its support leaves one traversal of its vertices. Left translation of one resulting list must agree with a cyclic rotation of the other, which captures the paper's change of basepoint.
* **Lemma 1.2** is `symmetric_figure_eight_lifts_of_abelian`. The kernel is normal and abelian. The graph downstairs uses the image of the generating set with the identity coset removed. The conclusion supplies a figure-eight upstairs and permits the lobe length to increase.
* **Lemma 1.3** is `two_power_cycle_lifts_of_normal_two_subgroup`. The kernel is a normal 2-subgroup of an arbitrary finite group. Again the image generating set excludes the identity coset. The exponents upstairs and downstairs are quantified independently. Neither lifting lemma imposes a lower bound on the size of the generating set.
* **Corollary 1.4** is `nilpotent_cayley_contains_two_power_cycle`. It replaces the odd-order assumption in Theorem 1.1 with finite nilpotence and includes the case of a 2-group.

The declarations cover the paper's four numbered results and its stated noncyclic strengthening. The Lean development proves these conclusions using the three permitted foundational axioms. The comparisons do not separately assert the paper's historical claims, its background bounds for general graphs, or every intermediate calculation.

## How the proofs correspond

**Lifting a figure-eight (§2, Lemma 1.2).** The paper chooses inverse-paired generator representatives and passes to a graph cover. Lean lifts two segments of the first lobe edge by edge under the quotient map. It translates the same segments and appends them in the opposite cyclic order to obtain the second lift. This gives the conjugacy of the endpoints used in the paper. Both arguments distinguish trivial, equal, inverse and remaining endpoints. In the last case Lean checks that one of the two commutator constructions has the required unique intersection. [Compatible lifts](CayleyErdosGyarfas/CompatibleLifts.lean) · [Endpoint cases](CayleyErdosGyarfas/FigureEightLifting.lean)

**Odd order (§2, Theorem 1.1 and its strengthening).** Both arguments start with a commuting square in the cyclic case and use a square and its diagonal translate in the noncyclic abelian case. Lean uses induction on group order for the paper's minimal-counterexample argument. It passes to a proper subgroup generated by a noncommuting pair or lifts a figure-eight from a smaller noncyclic quotient. Feit–Thompson supplies solvability. In the remaining case Lean constructs a faithful affine action using a minimal normal elementary abelian subgroup, a cyclic complement and the finite-field scalar model. The paper also describes this remaining group as Frobenius. [Induction](CayleyErdosGyarfas/MainReduction.lean) · [Field coordinates](CayleyErdosGyarfas/CyclicNormalFieldModel.lean) · [Odd-order proof](CayleyErdosGyarfas/OddOrderMain.lean)

**Lifting a cycle (§3, Lemma 1.3).** The paper treats generators in the kernel, collisions of generator images, and the graph-cover case. Lean proves a general lifting result for a surjective homomorphism from a finite group. Lift one traversal of the given cycle from `a` to `b`. The element `b a⁻¹` belongs to the kernel. Translating and joining copies of the lift produces a simple cycle whose length is the original length times the order of this element. In a 2-subgroup the multiplier is a power of two. This proof works with arbitrary multiplicities of generator images. [Cycle-lifting theorem](CayleyErdosGyarfas/Lifting.lean)

**Finite nilpotent groups (§3, Corollary 1.4).** Both arguments use a normal Sylow 2-subgroup. In a pure 2-group the paper distinguishes an all-involution generating set from one containing a generator of order at least four. Lean chooses two distinct, noninverse generators. A commuting pair gives a square, and a noncommuting pair gives an alternating cycle of length twice the order of their product. For a proper Sylow 2-subgroup, generators in the kernel and collisions between images give explicit cycles. When all images are distinct and nontrivial, the odd-order quotient retains the required degree and its cycle lifts.
